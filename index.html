      Robot cheat sheet
      • One wheel rotation rolls the robot 17.5 cm. 360 degrees is one rotation.
      • A quarter turn on the spot takes about 11 cm of wheel travel (226 degrees).
      • Which port does what is set under Robot setup. The fork arm lifts 90 degrees from down to all the way up.
      • The color sensor sits at the front right corner, looking down at the mat. A distance or force sensor, if you add one, faces forward.
      • A motor on a port with nothing attached in Robot setup still runs and counts degrees, so programs for other attachments don't stop.
      • Faint squares on the mat are 10 cm wide.
      • On Match Day: ▲ lift with the fork from underneath, ▼ lower the fork onto it, round knobs get pushed along their track.
      • After you set something down, back away with the fork still down before raising it, or you will pick it up again.
      • Drag a block back onto the block menu to delete it.

      Stuck on the field? What the rules say
      • Wait until the robot, and anything it is touching, is completely inside home. Then you can pick it up for free.
      • Touching it anywhere else is an interruption. You must bring it home and relaunch, and if it was even partly outside home you lose one precision token.
      • You start with 6 tokens. Tokens left at the end score: 6 = 50, 5 = 50, 4 = 35, 3 = 25, 2 = 15, 1 = 10, 0 = 0. So the first rescue costs nothing, and the next costs 15 points.
      • Partly outside home: it goes back to that same home. Completely outside: you choose either home.
      • Anything the robot picked up out on the field after its last launch goes to the referee.
      • If you are not going to launch again, you may stop the robot where it is with no token lost.
      • Points earned by interrupting the robot do not count.
